\((\Omega , \mathcal{F})\) will denote a space together with a sigma-algebra on it (a measurable space).

1 Definitions

Definition 1 Let \((\Omega , \mathcal{F}), \; (\Omega^\prime , \mathcal{F}^\prime )\) be two arbitrary measurable spaces. A map \(f: \Omega \to \Omega^\prime\) is called \(\mathcal{F} | \mathcal{F}^\prime\)-measurable (or simply measurable, if it is clear from context which sigma-algebras are meant) if the preimages of all elements of \(\mathcal{F}'\) lie in \(\mathcal{F}\):

\[ f^{-1}(B) \in \mathcal{F}, \qquad \forall B \in \mathcal{F}^\prime \]

This condition can be rewritten as \(f^{-1}(\mathcal{F}^\prime ) \subseteq \mathcal{F}\). The following notation is also used for \(f^{-1}(B)\): \(\left\{ f \in B\right\}\). A measurable function is also written as:

\[ f: (\Omega , \mathcal{F}) \to (\Omega ^\prime , \mathcal{F}^\prime ) \]

Lemma 1 (Measurability criterion) Suppose the sigma-algebra \(\mathcal{F}'\) on the codomain \(\Omega '\) is generated by some family \(\mathcal{A} \subset 2^{\Omega '}\): \(\mathcal{F}' = \sigma (\mathcal{A})\). Then \(f: \Omega \to \Omega '\) is measurable if and only if the preimages of the sets in \(\mathcal{A}\) lie in \(\mathcal{F}\): \(f^{-1}(\mathcal{A}) \subset \mathcal{F}\).

As a rule, we will be interested in numerical measurable maps, i.e., measurable functions. In this case \((\Omega^\prime , \mathcal{F}^\prime ) = (\overline{\mathbb {R}}, \mathscr {B}\left(\overline{\mathbb {R}}\right))\). It is convenient to take the extended real line as the codomain right away, since later on we will need, for example, to prove that the supremum of a collection of measurable functions is itself measurable. The open sets in \(\overline{\mathbb {R}} = [-\infty , +\infty ]\) are taken to be sets of the form

\[ A, \quad A \cup [-\infty , x), \quad A \cup (y, +\infty ], \quad A \cup [-\infty ,x) \cup (y, +\infty ] \]

where \(A \subset \mathbb {R}\) is open, \(x, y \in \mathbb {R}\). \(\mathscr {B}\left(\overline{\mathbb {R}}\right)\) is the Borel sigma-algebra on \(\overline{\mathbb {R}}\), i.e., the minimal sigma-algebra containing all open subsets of \(\overline{\mathbb {R}}\). It is not hard to prove that

\[ \begin{align} \mathscr {B}\left(\overline{\mathbb {R}}\right) = \mathscr {B}\left(\mathbb {R}\right) & \cup \left\{ B \cup \left\{ +\infty \right\} , B \in \mathscr {B}\left(\mathbb {R}\right)\right\} \cup \\ & \cup \left\{ B \cup \left\{ -\infty \right\} , B \in \mathscr {B}\left(\mathbb {R}\right)\right\} \cup \\ & \cup \left\{ B \cup \left\{ -\infty , +\infty \right\} , B \in \mathscr {B}\left(\mathbb {R}\right)\right\} \end{align} \]

From the measurability criterion and the properties of the Borel sigma-algebra on \(\mathbb {R}\) the following lemma follows.

Lemma 2 (measurability criterion for a function (a numerical map)) Let \(f: \Omega \to \mathbb {R}\), let \(\mathcal{F}\) be a sigma-algebra on \(\Omega\). Let \(D\) be a dense subset of \(\mathbb {R}\). The following conditions are equivalent:

  • \(f\) is \(\mathcal{F}\mid \mathscr {B}\left(\mathbb {R}\right)\)-measurable;

  • \(f^{-1}((x, +\infty ]) \in \mathcal{F}, \quad \forall x \in D\)

  • \(f^{-1}([x, +\infty ]) \in \mathcal{F}, \quad \forall x \in D\)

  • \(f^{-1}([-\infty , x)) \in \mathcal{F}, \quad \forall x \in D\)

  • \(f^{-1}([-\infty , x]) \in \mathcal{F}, \quad \forall x \in D\)

  • \(f^{-1}(B) \in \mathcal{F} \; \forall B \in \mathscr {B}\left(\mathbb {R}\right)\) and \(f^{-1}(\left\{ -\infty \right\} ), f^{-1}(\left\{ +\infty \right\} ) \in \mathcal{F}\).

Definition 2 A measurable function \(f: (\Omega , \mathcal{F}) \to (\overline{\mathbb {R}}, \mathscr {B}\left(\overline{\mathbb {R}}\right))\) is called Borel measurable if its domain \((\Omega , \mathcal{F})\) is a Borel subset of \(\mathbb {R}^{k}\) with the Borel sigma-algebra: \(\left(\Omega , \mathcal{F}\right) = \left(B, \mathscr {B}\left(B\right)\right)\), \(B \in \mathscr {B}\left(\mathbb {R}^{k}\right)\).

A measurable function \(f: (\Omega , \mathcal{F}) \to (\overline{\mathbb {R}}, \mathscr {B}\left(\overline{\mathbb {R}}\right))\) is called Lebesgue measurable if its domain is a Lebesgue measurable subset of \(\mathbb {R}^{k}\) with the sigma-algebra of its Lebesgue measurable subsets, i.e., \(\Omega = A \in \mathcal{M}(\lambda_k^*) \subset 2^{\mathbb {R}^{k}}\), \(\mathcal{F} = A \cap \mathcal{M}(\lambda^*_k)\).

2 Measurable functions and arithmetic operations

ProblemProblem 6

Let \(f: (\Omega , \mathcal{F}) \to (\overline{\mathbb {R}}, \mathscr {B}\left(\overline{\mathbb {R}}\right))\) be a measurable function. Prove that \[ h(\omega ) = \begin{cases} \frac{1}{f(\omega )}, & f(\omega ) \neq 0 \\ c, & f(\omega ) = 0 \end{cases} \] is measurable. Here \(c \in \mathbb {R}\) is an arbitrary constant.

ExampleExample 2

Let \(f,g: (\Omega , \mathcal{F}) \to (\overline{\mathbb {R}}, \mathscr {B}\left(\overline{\mathbb {R}}\right))\) be measurable functions. Prove that the event \(\left\{ f > g\right\}\) is measurable, i.e., that \[ \left\{ f > g\right\} = \left\{ \omega \; : \; f(\omega ) > g(\omega )\right\} \in \mathcal{F} \]

SolutionSolution

The set {f>g} as a countable union ⋃_{q∈ℚ}({f>q}∩{g<q}): move the slider q₀ and find a rational q₀ for which the point (f(ω₀),g(ω₀)) falls into the rectangle {f>q₀}∩{g<q₀} (it and the point turn green) — such a q₀ must exist, since the point lies above the diagonal f>g.

Note that

\[ \left\{ f > g\right\} = \left\{ \omega \; : \; f(\omega ) > g(\omega )\right\} = \bigcup _{q \in \mathbb {Q}} \bigg[\left\{ \omega \; : \; f(\omega ) > q\right\} \cap \left\{ \omega \; : \; q > g(\omega )\right\} \bigg] \]

Indeed, for an arbitrary \(\omega_0 \in \left\{ f > g\right\}\) there is a rational number \(q_0 = q(\omega_0)\) such that \(f(\omega_0) > q_0 > g(\omega_0)\) (since the rational numbers are dense in \(\overline{\mathbb {R}}\)). Hence

\[ \left\{ f > g\right\} \subset \bigcup _{q \in \mathbb {Q}} \bigg[\left\{ \omega \; : \; f(\omega ) > q\right\} \cap \left\{ \omega \; : \; q > g(\omega )\right\} \bigg] \]

The reverse inclusion

\[ \left\{ f > g\right\} \supset \bigcup _{q \in \mathbb {Q}} \bigg[\left\{ \omega \; : \; f(\omega ) > q\right\} \cap \left\{ \omega \; : \; q > g(\omega )\right\} \bigg] \]

is obvious.

We have

\[ \begin{align} \left\{ f > g\right\} & = \bigcup _{q \in \mathbb {Q}} \bigg[\left\{ \omega \; : \; f(\omega ) > q\right\} \cap \left\{ \omega \; : \; q > g(\omega )\right\} \bigg] = \\ & = \bigcup _{q \in \mathbb {Q}}\bigg[\underbrace{f^{-1}\left((q, +\infty ]\right)}_{\in \mathcal{F}} \cap \underbrace{g^{-1}\left([-\infty ,q)\right)}_{\in \mathcal{F}} \bigg] \in \mathcal{F} \end{align} \]

We used the measurability of \(f\), \(g\), as well as the closedness of the sigma-algebra under countable intersections.

3 Sequences of measurable functions

Theorem 1 Let \(f_n: (\Omega , \mathcal{F}) \to (\overline{\mathbb {R}}, \mathscr {B}\left(\overline{\mathbb {R}}\right)), n \in \mathbb {N}\) be a sequence of \(\mathcal{F}|\mathscr {B}\left(\overline{\mathbb {R}}\right)\)-measurable functions. Then

  1. The functions

\[ \sup _n f_n, \quad \inf _n f_n, \quad \varliminf _{n \to \infty } f_n, \quad \varlimsup _{n \to \infty } f_n \]

are \(\mathcal{F}|\mathscr {B}\left(\overline{\mathbb {R}}\right)\)-measurable;

  1. \(\left\{ \exists \lim_{n \to \infty }f_n\right\} = \left\{ \omega \in \Omega : \lim_{n\to \infty }f_n(\omega ) \text{ exists}\right\} \in \mathcal{F}\);

  2. Let

\[ h(\omega ) := \begin{cases} \lim \limits _{n\to \infty }f_n(\omega ), & \lim \limits _{n\to \infty }f_n(\omega )\text{ exists} \\ c, & \text{ otherwise}\end{cases} \]

where \(c \in \overline{\mathbb {R}}\) is an arbitrary fixed number. Then the function \(h\) is \(\mathcal{F}|\mathscr {B}\left(\overline{\mathbb {R}}\right)\)-measurable.

ExampleExample 3

Prove item 2 of the theorem.

HintHint

Use the Cauchy criterion.

SolutionSolution

We have

\[ \begin{align} \left\{ \exists \lim \limits _{n \to \infty } f_n\right\} & = \left\{ \omega \in \Omega : \lim _{n\to \infty }f_n(\omega ) \text{ exists}\right\} = \\ & = \left\{ \forall k \in \mathbb {N} \; \exists N \in \mathbb {N}\; :\; \forall m,n \geq N \hookrightarrow \left|f_n - f_m\right| < \frac{1}{k}\right\} = \\ & =\bigcap _{k=1}^{\infty }\left\{ \exists N\; :\; \forall m,n \geq N \hookrightarrow \left|f_n - f_m\right| < \frac{1}{k}\right\} = \\ & = \bigcap _{k=1}^{\infty } \bigcup _{N=1}^{\infty } \bigcap _{m,n = N}^{\infty } \left\{ \left|f_n - f_m\right| < \frac{1}{k}\right\} = \\ & = \bigcap _{k=1}^{\infty } \bigcup _{N=1}^{\infty } \bigcap _{m,n = N}^{\infty } \left(\left\{ f_{n} - f_{m} < \frac{1}{k}\right\} \cap \left\{ f_{n} - f_{m} > -\frac{1}{k}\right\} \right) \end{align} \]

(a universal quantifier turns into an intersection, an existential quantifier turns into a union). Next, the sets

\[ \left\{ f_{n} - f_{m} < \frac{1}{k}\right\} , \left\{ f_{n} - f_{m} > -\frac{1}{k}\right\} \in \mathcal{F} \]

i.e., they are measurable (see Example 2, where this is proved). \(\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty } \bigcup_{N=1}^{\infty } \bigcap_{m,n = N}^{\infty }\) are countable intersections and unions. Hence,

\[ \left\{ \exists \lim \limits _{n \to \infty } f_n\right\} \in \mathcal{F} \]

It is important that all unions and intersections range over at most countable collections of events.

4 “Almost everywhere”

Definition 3 Let \((\Omega , \mathcal{F}, \mu )\) be a measure space. We say that some property holds for almost all outcomes \(\omega\) (also said to hold \(\mu\)-almost everywhere, abbreviated \(\mu\)-a.e. or simply a.e.), if the measure of the outcomes where the property fails is zero. For example, one can say that almost all numbers in \(\mathbb {R}\) (taking Lebesgue measure on \(\mathbb {R}\) unless stated otherwise) are irrational, since the measure of the rational numbers is zero.

5 2 new modes of convergence for functions: almost everywhere and in measure

In this section, instead of the extended real line \(\overline{\mathbb {R}}\) the functions will take values in \(\mathbb {R}\), in order to avoid indeterminate forms such as \(+\infty - (+\infty )\).

Definition 4 Let \((\Omega , \mathcal{F}, \mu )\) be a measure space. Let \(f, f_1, f_2, \dots : (\Omega , \mathcal{F}) \to (\mathbb {R}, \mathscr {B}\left(\mathbb {R}\right))\) be a collection of \(\mathcal{F}|\mathscr {B}\left(\mathbb {R}\right)\)-measurable functions. We say that \(f_n\) converges to \(f\) almost everywhere with respect to the measure \(\mu\), or \(\mu\)-a.e. (or simply a.e., if it is clear which measure is meant), if \[ \mu \left(\left\{ \lim f_n \neq f\right\} \right) = \mu \left(\left\{ \omega \in \Omega : \lim _{n \to \infty } f_n(\omega ) \neq f(\omega )\right\} \right) = 0 \] The notation “\(\lim_{n \to \infty } f_n(\omega ) \neq f(\omega )\)” means that either the limit does not exist, or it exists but is not equal to \(f(\omega )\).

For this definition to be well posed we need the measurability of \(\left\{ \lim f_n \neq f\right\}\), i.e., we need \(\left\{ \omega \in \Omega : \lim_{n \to \infty } f_n(\omega ) \neq f(\omega )\right\} \in \mathcal{F}\). Note that

\[ \begin{align} \left\{ \omega \in \Omega : \lim _{n \to \infty } f_n(\omega ) = f(\omega )\right\} & = \left\{ \omega \in \Omega : \; \forall m \in \mathbb {N} \, \exists N \in \mathbb {N} \, \forall n \geq N \, \hookrightarrow \, \left|f(\omega ) - f_{n}(\omega )\right| \leq \frac{1}{m}\right\} = \\ & = \bigcap _{m=1}^\infty \bigcup _{N=1}^\infty \bigcap _{n=N}^\infty \underbrace{\left\{ \omega : \left|f(\omega ) - f_n(\omega )\right| \leq \frac{1}{m}\right\} }_{\in \mathcal{F}} \in \mathcal{F} \end{align} \]

Hence,

\[ \left\{ \lim f_n \neq f\right\} = \left\{ \lim f_n = f\right\} ^{\complement } \in \mathcal{F} \]

Definition 5 Let \((\Omega , \mathcal{F}, \mu )\) be a measure space. Let \(f, f_1, f_2, \dots : (\Omega , \mathcal{F}) \to (\mathbb {R}, \mathscr {B}\left(\mathbb {R}\right))\) be a collection of \(\mathcal{F}|\mathscr {B}\left(\mathbb {R}\right)\)-measurable functions. We say that \(f_n\) converges to \(f\) in measure \(\mu\) if \[ \mu \left(\omega : \left|f_n(\omega ) - f(\omega )\right| > \varepsilon \right) \xrightarrow [n \to \infty ]{} 0, \quad \forall \varepsilon > 0 \]

In probability theory, as you may recall, convergence in measure was called convergence in probability. Convergence in probability is a special case of convergence in measure, in which the measure is a probability measure.

6 Egorov’s theorem

A pointwise convergent sequence of functions need not converge uniformly. However, the following result asserts that a pointwise convergent sequence of functions on a measure space of finite total measure converges almost uniformly, in the sense that it converges uniformly except on a set that can be made to have arbitrarily small measure.

Theorem 4 (D. Egorov) Let \(\mu \left(\Omega \right) < +\infty\) and let \(f_n\) converge to \(f\) almost everywhere. Then \(\forall \varepsilon > 0\) there exists \(B_\varepsilon \in \mathcal{F}\) such that \(f_n \to f\) uniformly on \(B_\varepsilon\) and \(\mu \left(B_\varepsilon^\complement \right) < \varepsilon\).

ProblemProblem 20

7 Lebesgue measurable functions

The passage from Borel measurable functions to Lebesgue measurable functions is the work of the devil. Don’t even think about it!

— Barry Simon (winner of the American Mathematical Society’s Steele Prize) in his five-volume “A Comprehensive Course in Analysis”

Although there exist Lebesgue measurable sets that are not Borel, you are unlikely to encounter them. Likewise, a Lebesgue measurable function that is not Borel measurable is unlikely to arise in your work. An excellent way to avoid potential confusion is to consider only Borel measurable functions.

The following result asserts that if we adhere to the philosophy that what happens on a set of outer measure \(0\) does not matter much, then we may restrict our attention to Borel measurable functions.

Theorem 5 (every Lebesgue measurable function is almost Borel measurable.) Suppose \(f: \mathbb {R} \rightarrow \mathbb {R}\) is a Lebesgue measurable function. Then there exists a Borel measurable function \(g: \mathbb {R} \rightarrow \mathbb {R}\) such that \[ \lambda \left(g \neq f\right) = \lambda \left(\left\{ x \in \mathbb {R} \; : \; g(x) \neq f(x)\right\} \right) = 0 \]
